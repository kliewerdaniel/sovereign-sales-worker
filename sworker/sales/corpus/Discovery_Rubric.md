# Discovery Rubric

How we turn observed evidence into a qualified opportunity. Every sub-score below
is derived from stored evidence, never from a model's mood.

## Pain-Point Opportunity Formula

Each detected pain point is scored with the rubric formula (normalised 0–100):

    (Pain × Frequency × Revenue Impact × Automation Potential)
        / Implementation Difficulty

Where each dimension is 1–5, observed from real source text:

- **Pain / Severity** — how much the problem hurts operations
- **Frequency** — how often it recurs (daily = 5, quarterly = 1)
- **Revenue Impact** — how directly it costs or risks money
- **Automation Potential** — how cleanly a local AI system can remove it
- **Implementation Difficulty** — 1 (trivial) to 5 (hard); divides the product

## Signal Categories

Detected signals map to these categories:

- **Lead Capture / Intake** — manual entry, re-keying, copy-paste between systems
- **Knowledge Flow** — answers living in one person's head; no searchable source
- **Document Flow** — contracts, reports, intake packets assembled by hand
- **Reporting** — repetitive reporting nobody reads but everyone produces
- **Coverage** — work that slips outside business hours or between owners
- **Tooling** — many overlapping SaaS tools doing what one owned system could

## Tiers (see Hypothesis_Log.md)

Observed > Inferred > Hypothesized. A signal match is OBSERVED. A reasonable
conclusion from it is INFERRED. Something that needs the prospect to validate is
HYPOTHESIZED and must be phrased as a question, never stated as fact.
