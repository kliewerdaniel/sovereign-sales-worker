# Ideal Customer Profile — Industry Ranking

Consulting practice: **Sovereign AI Systems Engineering**. We help businesses
build practical, local-first AI systems they control — instead of handing their
data and workflows to increasingly expensive cloud AI services.

Ranking scores each industry 0–5 on fit with the offer: how much repetitive,
document- and knowledge-heavy work they carry, how dependent they are on
expensive SaaS, and how much they stand to gain from private, owned AI
infrastructure. The top-ranked industry is the default active ICP.

### 1. Professional Services Firms — Score: 5/5

10–200 person consultancies, agencies, and boutique firms. Heavy knowledge work,
proposal writing, client reporting, and fragmented SaaS stacks. High willingness
to pay for leverage; sensitive to client-data privacy.

### 2. Law & Accounting Practices — Score: 4.5/5

Document-heavy, repetition-heavy, privacy-critical. Strong fit for local-first
knowledge retrieval, document automation, and internal research assistants.
Regulated — privacy is the buying trigger, not a nice-to-have.

### 3. Manufacturing & Trades SMBs — Score: 4/5

Operations, quoting, and internal knowledge scattered across spreadsheets and
tribal memory. Good fit for workflow automation and an internal knowledge system
that does not leak IP to a cloud vendor.

### 4. Healthcare & Clinics (non-patient-data-heavy ops) — Score: 3.5/5

Scheduling, billing ops, and documentation. Privacy (HIPAA-adjacent) makes
local-first a differentiator. Scope carefully; avoid clinical-decision use.

### 5. SaaS-Dependent SMBs — Score: 3/5

Companies paying for many overlapping SaaS tools to do what one owned workflow
system could. The "reduce software spend" angle is strong; must show ROI vs.
existing stack.

### 6. Internal Ops / RevOps Teams — Score: 3.5/5

Cross-functional operations teams inside larger orgs who own reporting,
onboarding, and internal tooling. Good for internal AI assistants and
automation of repetitive reporting.

### 7. Agencies & Content Studios — Score: 3/5

Content production bottlenecks, client reporting, repetitive creative ops. Fit
for content pipelines and local generative assistants.
